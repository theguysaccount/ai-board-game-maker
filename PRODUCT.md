# Product: Printable Game Maker

**User.** A teacher who wants a review game for Friday, a parent planning a birthday or shower, a scout leader before a campout. Secondary: homeschool families and tutors.

**Painful job.** "Turn the words we're learning (or the in-jokes we share) into a game people actually want to play, tonight, without buying or designing anything." Today: hand-drawn bingo grids, paid template sites, or a search for someone else's worksheet that never quite matches the list.

**Promise (5 seconds).** *Paste a word list, pick a game, print it. Eight original games, free PDFs, nothing to sign up for.*

**The wedge.**
1. **Search for the topic, not the tool.** 34 topic pages at launch (one more a week) answer "printable [topic] bingo/game" with a real, checked list and instant PDFs.
2. **One list, eight games.** A teacher who comes for bingo leaves with the board game, loop cards and quiz too: a week of activities from one list.
3. **Every printed page carries the link** (small, removable), the rules page says "change the words and print again for free", and share links reopen the exact game for a colleague.
4. **Play on screen** (bingo caller, memory vs computer) gives a reason to come back in class.

**Why now.** Holiday parties and end-of-term reviews peak in November and December; the holiday packs are live for it.

**Activation metric.** A PDF download (`pdf_download`). Instrumented; switched on by the GoatCounter key.

**First revenue event.** Someone ordering a professionally printed, boxed copy of their game through the print-on-demand slot (built, off until hosting moves off GitHub Pages; see OPERATIONS.md key 2).

**Settled questions.** *Gift product or designer tool first?* Neither, first: the fastest stranger value is free classroom and party printables; the gift/boxed-copy path is the money key layered on top. *Partner with the linked printer or build fulfillment?* Partner (a print-on-demand game printer via the shop slot); never build fulfillment.

**v1 deliberately won't:** call AI at runtime; take payments; require accounts; copy or name any commercial game; support right-to-left scripts or emoji in PDFs (Latin script, with accents, is covered by the embedded fonts).
